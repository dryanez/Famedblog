The Anamnesegespräch is where the FaMed exam starts to feel like a real ward. You sit opposite a patient, played by an actor, who has come in with a complaint. Your job is to find out what is wrong and what lies behind it, and to make the person feel heard while you do it. Nobody is testing whether you know the right antibiotic. The examiners want to hear whether you can run a doctor–patient conversation in German that is clear, polite and well organised.

That is the idea behind the admission interview in our book *FaMED Protokoll* (Yanez & Maraqa). Use one reliable structure for every case: greet, take the personal details, explore the main complaint, then work through the background in a fixed order and close with a short outlook. Once the structure is automatic, your attention is free for what matters most, which is listening.

This article sets out that structure in tables you can practise with.

## The task at a glance

| Aspect | What applies |
|---|---|
| Where it sits | Station 1, the doctor–patient station: the history interview plus an informed-consent conversation ([official exam flow](https://famed-test.de/pruefungsablauf/)) |
| Time | The oral block lasts 23 minutes including reading. In the [current practice test](https://famed-test.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FaMed-Uebungstest_2-1.pdf), each oral situation takes at most 11 minutes |
| Your role | The doctor taking the history. Read the assignment for your role, the setting and the person you are speaking with |
| What is assessed | Professional language: understandable questions, appropriate register, structure, and responses to the patient. It is not a medical-knowledge test |
| Pass threshold | At least 60 % in each of the three stations; a weak station cannot be offset by a stronger one |
| What it is not | A checklist recital. OPQRST and SAMPLER are memory aids, not published scoring rubrics |

Don't assume the history case carries over into the consent task or the written task. Treat each assignment as it is written.

## What the examiner wants to hear

1. **Structure.** A logical order from the main complaint to the background, with transitions you announce.
2. **Understandable German.** Short questions in everyday words. Translate the answers into medical terms in your head.
3. **Interpersonal skill.** An introduction, consent to the conversation, responses to fear or pain, and no premature diagnosis.

## The conversation step by step

This order follows the general admission interview in *FaMED Protokoll*. Adapt it to the case: if the patient has already answered something, don't ask it again just to tick a box.

| Step | Goal | Example German phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Greeting and role | Introduce yourself and get agreement to the conversation | „Guten Tag, ich bin Assistenzarzt hier auf der Station und würde gerne das Aufnahmegespräch mit Ihnen führen. Sind Sie damit einverstanden?“ |
| 2. Ground rules | Invite the patient to stop you when something is unclear | „Wenn Sie etwas nicht verstehen, geben Sie mir bitte ein Zeichen.“ |
| 3. Personal details | Name (spelled out), age, date of birth, height, weight, job, GP | „Können Sie mir bitte Ihren Nachnamen buchstabieren?“ |
| 4. Main complaint | Open question, then let the patient talk | „Was führt Sie zu uns?“ |
| 5. Current complaint | Location, onset, course, character, intensity, triggers, radiation | „Wie stark sind die Schmerzen auf einer Skala von 1 bis 10?“ |
| 6. Associated symptoms | Questions specific to the complaint (e.g. cough, swallowing, dizziness) | „Ist der Husten trocken oder mit Auswurf?“ |
| 7. B symptoms and vegetative history | Fever, night sweats, weight, appetite, sleep, bowels and bladder | „Haben Sie in den letzten sechs Monaten ungewollt abgenommen?“ |
| 8. Previous illnesses and operations | Chronic illnesses, operations, complications | „Wurden Sie schon einmal operiert? Gab es Komplikationen?“ |
| 9. Medication | Regular and as-needed medication, with dose | „Nehmen Sie regelmäßig Medikamente ein? Welche und in welcher Dosierung?“ |
| 10. Allergies | Allergies and intolerances, and how they show | „Sind bei Ihnen Allergien bekannt? Wie äußert sich das?“ |
| 11. Family history | Chronic illnesses in the family | „Gibt es in Ihrer Familie Erkrankungen wie Diabetes oder Krebs?“ |
| 12. Substances (Noxen) | Smoking, alcohol, drugs, quantified where possible | „Wie viele Zigaretten rauchen Sie am Tag?“ |
| 13. Close | Open question, summary, next steps | „Möchten Sie noch etwas hinzufügen?“ |

For pain, [OPQRST can help you organise steps 5 and 6](/blog/pain-anamnesis-guide). Use it as a memory aid. Don't recite it.

### Close without promising anything

The book ends the interview with an outlook. The patient hears what comes next: a physical examination, a blood sample, and a discussion with the senior physician. For example:

> Ich habe jetzt alle wichtigen Informationen. Als Nächstes würde ich Sie gern körperlich untersuchen und Ihnen etwas Blut abnehmen. Danach bespreche ich die Ergebnisse mit meinem Oberarzt. Haben Sie noch Fragen an mich?

Give a short summary before that: „Ich fasse kurz zusammen: … Habe ich Sie richtig verstanden?“

## Everyday words vs. medical terms

Patients speak in everyday language. Your notes and the later case presentation need the medical term. Practise both directions: ask in plain words, then think in the medical term.

| The patient says | Medical term | English |
|---|---|---|
| Verstopfung | Obstipation | Constipation |
| Durchfall | Diarrhö | Diarrhoea |
| Blut im Stuhl | Hämatochezie | Blood in the stool |
| Schluckbeschwerden | Dysphagie | Difficulty swallowing |
| Sodbrennen | Pyrosis | Heartburn |
| Auswurf | Sputum | Phlegm |
| Blut beim Husten | Hämoptyse | Coughing up blood |
| Atemnot | Dyspnoe | Shortness of breath |
| Schwindel | Vertigo | Dizziness |
| Ohrgeräusche | Tinnitus | Ringing in the ears |
| Mandelentzündung | Tonsillitis | Tonsillitis |
| Lichtempfindlichkeit | Photophobie | Sensitivity to light |
| Kribbeln, Taubheitsgefühl | Parästhesie | Pins and needles, numbness |
| Fieber, Nachtschweiß, Gewichtsverlust | B-Symptomatik | B symptoms |
| Magenspiegelung | Gastroskopie | Gastroscopy |

Patients often use idioms, such as „Mir liegt etwas schwer im Magen“. Our [guide to patient idioms](/blog/deutsche-patientensprache-redewendungen) helps you practise asking what they mean.

## Reacting to the patient

The actor will often show feelings or ask questions you cannot answer yet. *FaMED Protokoll* gives each of these situations its own reaction. Here is a selection, shortened and arranged by purpose:

| Situation | Purpose | Example German phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Patient is afraid | Empathy | „Ich kann Ihre Angst sehr gut verstehen. Sie sind hier in guten Händen.“ |
| Patient is stressed by the symptoms | Reassurance | „Es war eine gute Entscheidung, dass Sie zu uns gekommen sind.“ |
| Patient is in pain during the conversation | Show care, offer a choice | „Können Sie die Schmerzen noch kurz aushalten, oder soll ich Ihnen jetzt ein Schmerzmittel geben lassen?“ |
| Answer is vague | Clarify | „Wenn Sie sagen, Ihnen ist schwindelig: Wie fühlt sich das genau an?“ |
| Asks for the diagnosis | Be honest about what is known so far | „Ich verstehe, dass Sie die Ursache schnell wissen möchten. Dafür brauchen wir noch die Untersuchung und Laborwerte.“ |
| Fears cancer | Avoid both extremes | „Es kann viele Ursachen für Ihre Beschwerden geben. Wir klären das gemeinsam ab.“ |
| Asks how long the stay will be | Don't give a false date | „Das kann ich Ihnen im Moment noch nicht sagen. Zuerst brauchen wir einige Untersuchungen.“ |
| Worried about an examination | Explain its purpose | „Das ist eine Routineuntersuchung, die wir brauchen, um eine Diagnose stellen zu können.“ |
| Wants the senior physician | Show respect, explain the team | „Das verstehe ich gut. Wir besprechen jeden Fall im Team mit dem Oberarzt.“ |
| Getting impatient | Ask for understanding | „Ich brauche noch ein paar Informationen, damit wir Ihnen gezielt helfen können. Ich bitte um Ihr Verständnis.“ |
| End of the conversation | Summarise | „Ich fasse kurz zusammen: … Habe ich Sie richtig verstanden?“ |

Don't make promises nobody can keep. Empathy and honesty belong together.

## Common mistakes and what to do instead

| Common mistake | Better |
|---|---|
| Going straight into a list of questions without introducing yourself | Name, role, consent to the conversation, then „Was führt Sie zu uns?“ |
| Several questions in one sentence | One clear question at a time |
| Using medical terms with the patient („Haben Sie Dyspnoe?“) | Everyday words („Bekommen Sie schlecht Luft?“), and use the term later in the presentation |
| Asking again about something already answered | Pick it up instead: „Sie sagten bereits, dass Sie … nehmen. Nehmen Sie noch andere Medikamente?“ |
| Ignoring fear or pain | Name it briefly and respond, then carry on |
| Smoking or alcohol without quantities | Ask for amount and duration: cigarettes per day, how many years, drinks per week |
| Promising a diagnosis or a "definitely harmless" result | Name the next steps: examination, lab tests, discussion with the senior physician |
| Jumping between topics | Announce transitions: „Jetzt habe ich noch einige Fragen zu Ihren Vorerkrankungen.“ |

## How to practise

Learn the 13 steps until you can run through them without notes. Then practise with a partner who plays the patient and works in one reaction from the table, such as fear or impatience. End every round with a summary and an outlook.

For a focused partner session, try the [three follow-up question cards with patient replies](/blog/famed-anamnese-follow-up-question-drill). The consent part of the same station is covered in the [guide to the informed-consent conversation](/blog/famed-aufklaerungsgespraech-guide).
