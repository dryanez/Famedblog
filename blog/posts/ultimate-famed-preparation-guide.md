FaMED preparation starts with understanding the task you are preparing for: professional medical-language communication.

## Start with the official format

The exam provider's published practice test includes patient-history and informed-consent conversations, doctor-to-doctor patient presentations, and written documentation. Consult the [official FaMed practice test](https://famed-test.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FaMed-Uebungstest_2-1.pdf) and the provider's current information for the applicable instructions. This preparation site is independent of the exam provider.

A study schedule is not an examination rulebook. Check the current format, eligibility, timing and registration information with the provider rather than relying on an older blog post.

## Choose one communication goal

Start with a task you can actually practise: opening a conversation, summarising information, asking a clear follow-up question, presenting a case or drafting a section of a letter. Write down the exact skill you want to improve.

If you want to practise a patient presentation, use the [SBAR article](/blog/sbar-schema-arzt-arzt-gespraech) as a preparation framework. It does not replace the instructions for your specific exam task.

## Combine reading and speaking

Read a short section, close the material and explain the idea in your own words. Record your own practice if you find it useful. Listen for clarity and structure, then repeat the most difficult passage. Qualified feedback is valuable when you need to check medical content or recurring language problems.

## Make writing a regular task

Work on one section of a medical letter at a time. Identify the information supplied in the task, organise it and write a clear draft. Compare it with the task requirements and revise one issue at a time. Our [written-anamnesis article](/blog/famed-arztbrief-soap-schema) provides a starting point for that practice.

## Build a sustainable weekly schedule

Set a realistic number of sessions around your work and language-learning commitments. Mix reading, speaking, writing and review rather than measuring progress only by pages read. Use the [eight-week study plan](/blog/8-week-famed-study-plan) as a template, not a promise that every learner needs the same amount of time. The [30-minute routine](/blog/famed-vorbereitung-30-minuten-lernroutine) is useful when time is limited.

## Your next step

Choose one task today and repeat it after feedback. A smaller completed practice session gives you something concrete to review. Use book previews, app demonstrations and the relevant articles above to choose resources that fit that task.
