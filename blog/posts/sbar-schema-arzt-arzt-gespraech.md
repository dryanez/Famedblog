In the Arzt–Arzt part of FaMED you are the doctor who knows the patient, and the person opposite you does not. You receive written case information: patient data, the reason for admission, history, findings and often a plan. Your job is to pass it on to a colleague in clear, professional German, the way you would in a ward round or a handover outside the patient's room.

A colleague listening to a presentation needs three things. They need to know **who** the patient is and **why** they are here. They need the **relevant** history and findings, not every line from the sheet. And they need to know **what is being suspected and what happens next**. A good presentation moves in that order: from the person and the complaint, through what was found, to an assessment and a plan. Then it is open for questions.

Most of the language work is turning the patient's words ("Atemnot", "Auswurf") into medical terms ("Dyspnoe", "Sputum"), reporting what was said in reported speech, and linking facts so the listener can follow your reasoning. FaMed is a professional-language exam. This station tests how you communicate the supplied case. It does not test whether you can recall a treatment algorithm from memory.

## The task at a glance

| Aspect | What applies |
|---|---|
| Station | Doctor–doctor communication: **two separate case presentations** |
| Time | **23 minutes including reading** for the oral block. The current practice test allows **at most 11 minutes per situation** |
| Your source | The written case information you are given, and nothing else |
| Your output | A structured oral presentation in professional German, followed by answers to follow-up questions |
| Pass requirement | Each of the three stations needs at least 60%. There is no compensation between stations |
| Not required | Recalling therapy from memory, adding findings, or using one fixed official template |

Treat the two cases as completely independent. Don't assume anything carries over from one case to the other.

## A presentation structure that flows

This order follows the logic used in the FaMED Protokoll book. SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) covers the same ground in four blocks. Either approach works as a **practice aid**. Neither one is an official scoring rubric. If a case has nothing for a section, skip it. Don't fill it with guesses.

| Part | Content | Example German phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Patient data | Name, age, setting, reason for presenting | „Ich möchte Ihnen Herrn Becker vorstellen, einen 62-jährigen Patienten, der mit der Verdachtsdiagnose einer COPD-Exazerbation stationär aufgenommen wurde.“ |
| Chief complaint | Main symptom, onset, course | „Der Patient gab an, seit drei Tagen unter zunehmender Dyspnoe zu leiden.“ |
| History | Previous illnesses, medication, allergies, risk factors, social history | „Anamnestisch bestehe eine langjährige COPD. Allergien seien nicht bekannt.“ |
| Findings | Vital signs, examination, laboratory results, imaging, all as supplied | „Die klinische Untersuchung zeigt eine Tachypnoe mit einer Atemfrequenz von 29/min.“ |
| Suspected diagnosis | The working diagnosis given or supported by the case | „Es besteht der Verdacht auf eine akute Exazerbation der COPD.“ |
| Differentials | Alternatives, if the case calls for them | „Differentialdiagnostisch kommt eine Pneumonie in Betracht.“ |
| Plan | What has been done, what is planned and what is recommended | „Eine kalkulierte antibiotische Therapie wurde bereits eingeleitet.“ |
| Questions | Invite the listener, then answer precisely | „Haben Sie dazu noch Fragen?“ |

There is a grammar point behind the history row. What the patient *reported* usually goes into reported speech (Konjunktiv I: „er habe“, „sie leide“, „es bestünden“). What you *measured or found* stays in the indicative („die Untersuchung zeigt“, „der CRP-Wert war erhöht“). That contrast tells the listener which facts are reported and which are objective.

## Linking language: cause, condition, time and certainty

A list of facts becomes a presentation when you connect them. You also have to signal clearly how certain each statement is.

| Function | German phrase | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Causal | „Aufgrund von … wurde … eingeleitet.“ | A measure that follows from a finding |
| Causal | „Da die Patientin unter einer Hypoxämie leidet, besteht die Indikation für …“ | Giving a reason |
| Conditional | „Falls sich der Zustand nicht verbessert, wird … durchgeführt.“ | A plan that depends on the course |
| Conditional | „Sollte eine Verschlechterung eintreten, ist … vorgesehen.“ | A contingency in the supplied plan |
| Temporal | „Nach der Aufnahme wurde …“ | Order of events |
| Temporal | „Während des stationären Aufenthalts …“ | Something that happened over a period |
| Temporal | „Seit drei Tagen bestünden …“ | Symptom duration in reported speech |
| Hedging | „Es besteht der Verdacht auf …“ | A suspected diagnosis that is not confirmed |
| Hedging | „… vermutlich ausgelöst durch …“ | A probable cause |
| Hedging | „… ist nicht auszuschließen.“ | A possibility that remains open |
| Nuanced | „Es zeigt sich …“ / „Es liegt … vor.“ | A confirmed finding or diagnosis |
| Planning | „… ist geplant / wird empfohlen / wird in Betracht gezogen.“ | Separating a plan, a recommendation and an option |
| Missing data | „Dazu liegen mir keine Angaben vor.“ | Information the case does not contain |

Pay special attention to the last two rows. „Wurde durchgeführt“, „ist geplant“ and „wird empfohlen“ mean three different things. If you mix them up, you change the facts of the case.

## A short model presentation

This example is adapted from a respiratory practice case. It is shortened to show the flow and is not a complete answer:

> Ich möchte Ihnen Herrn Becker vorstellen, einen 62-jährigen Patienten, der mit der Verdachtsdiagnose einer akuten COPD-Exazerbation stationär aufgenommen wurde. Er gab an, seit drei Tagen unter zunehmender Dyspnoe sowie produktivem Husten zu leiden; nach Anwendung seines Salbutamol-Sprays sei eine leichte Besserung eingetreten. Anamnestisch bestehe eine langjährige COPD, er sei aktiver Raucher. Die klinische Untersuchung zeigt eine Tachypnoe und eine Sauerstoffsättigung von 89 % unter Raumluft, auskultatorisch ein verlängertes Exspirium mit Giemen. Die Entzündungsparameter sind erhöht. Es besteht der Verdacht auf eine infektgetriggerte Exazerbation. Angaben zu einer Röntgenaufnahme liegen mir nicht vor.

Look at what the model does. It opens with the person and the reason for admission. Reported complaints are in Konjunktiv I and findings are in the indicative. It flags the suspected diagnosis as suspected, and it states openly that something is missing.

## Common mistakes and better versions

| Mistake | Better |
|---|---|
| Presenting a finding that isn't on the sheet: „Die Lunge war ansonsten frei.“ | State what is documented. If you are asked about something that isn't there: „Dazu liegen mir keine Angaben vor.“ |
| Turning a missing medication history into a negative one: „Er nimmt keine Medikamente.“ | „Eine Medikamentenanamnese ist in den Unterlagen nicht dokumentiert.“ |
| Presenting a suspicion as a fact: „Der Patient hat eine Pneumonie.“ | „Es besteht der Verdacht auf eine Pneumonie.“ |
| Presenting a plan as already done: „Er wurde operiert.“ | „Eine Operation ist geplant.“ |
| Reading the sheet out line by line | Select what is relevant, group it by section and link it with „zudem“, „aufgrund“, „im Verlauf“ |
| Using lay words throughout: „Er hat Atemnot und Auswurf.“ | „Er klagt über Dyspnoe und produktiven Husten.“ |
| Mixing details from the first and second case | Keep separate notes, finish one case completely, and check the name, age and complaint before you start the next |
| Adding treatment doses from memory | Present only the therapy that the case supplies |
| Ending abruptly | Close with the plan and invite questions: „Haben Sie dazu noch Fragen?“ |

## Practise two cases independently

Make separate notes for each practice case. After speaking, compare your recording with the source. Did you mix up identities, times, findings or plans? Then repeat only the section that was unclear. To practise degrees of certainty, use the [differential-diagnosis phrasing exercise](/blog/differentialdiagnose-formulieren-deutsch).

The [written station](/blog/famed-arztbrief-soap-schema) is a different task: you write an anamnesis in complete sentences from a patient video. Don't carry facts from a presentation into it.

A quick self-check after each attempt:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Identity | Name, age and sex correct? |
| Complaint | Symptom and duration as supplied? |
| Certainty | Suspected vs confirmed, planned vs done? |
| Gaps | Missing information named as missing, not filled in? |

This is a self-check, not the official grading rubric.
