Ask clear questions about the person's actual complaint, listen to the response and preserve important information.

FaMed assesses medical-language communication; medical knowledge is not separately evaluated. The following are original language exercises, not a complete emergency assessment or an official scoring checklist.

## Clarify the beginning and course

For a fictional pain-history exercise:

- „Wann haben die Beschwerden begonnen?“
- „Haben sie plötzlich eingesetzt oder allmählich zugenommen?“
- „Was war in diesem Moment anders als sonst?“

Use a clear follow-up when the answer is vague. „Seit gestern“ does not yet explain whether symptoms began suddenly or gradually.

## Ask one associated-symptom question at a time

Examples include „Haben Sie zusätzlich Atemnot bemerkt?“ or „Sind Sprach- oder Sehstörungen hinzugekommen?“. Choose according to the scenario rather than delivering an indiscriminate list.

Asking a question does not establish or rule out a diagnosis. Nor does the absence of one reported symptom justify saying that a serious condition is excluded.

## Distinguish a denial from missing information

These statements mean different things:

- „Die Patientin verneine Fieber.“ — she explicitly denied it.
- „Eine Temperaturmessung ist nicht angegeben.“ — no value was supplied.
- „Fieber wurde in dieser Übung nicht erfragt.“ — the question was not asked.

Do not write „B-Symptomatik verneint“ unless the relevant questions were actually answered. [The B-symptom article](/blog/b-symptoms-explained) shows how to ask about fever, sweating and weight change separately.

## Summarise without false reassurance

> Ich möchte kurz prüfen, ob ich Sie richtig verstanden habe: … Welche weitere Veränderung ist Ihnen aufgefallen?

If a practice scenario calls for an explanation of next steps, use its supplied plan. Do not invent a reassuring diagnosis or promise that a symptom is harmless.
