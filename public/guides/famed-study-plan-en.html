Eight-week FaMED study plan

Save this page as a PDF from your browser’s Print menu.

This is a suggested study schedule, not an official timetable or a passing guarantee. Adjust it to your language level, available time and feedback. Do not leave writing until the last week.

The current human-medicine format has three stations and five tasks: history, informed consent, two patient presentations and written anamnesis from a video. The oral blocks are each 23 minutes including reading; writing is 22 minutes including video. The practice test sets an 11-minute maximum for each oral situation.

For cases and useful phrases to practise alongside this topic, get the FaMED Protokoll digital book for €19.99. You can also read a sample first.

Weeks 1–2: Build a baseline across all tasks

Record a short fictional history exercise. Explain a supplied procedure in everyday language. Present a short case note and write an anamnesis paragraph from a video or transcript. Identify one weakness in each skill.

Use OPQRST for pain questions as a flexible memory aid. Keep a list of unclear phrases, not a list of supposedly guaranteed exam diagnoses.

An example study session: read the assignment, practise one section, compare it with the source and repeat the unclear part. Allocate the duration to your needs.

Weeks 3–4: Clear questions and understandable explanations

Practise follow-up questions, relevant medication/allergy history and concise patient-facing summaries. For consent, use supplied information about purpose, procedure, risks and behaviour before/after the intervention.

Our consent guide focuses on that task. Different exercises can involve different people; do not assume one story continues through the whole exam.

Continue short writing sessions several times a week. Preserve times and negations. Use complete sentences rather than filling a SOAP template with invented findings.

Weeks 5–6: Two case presentations and source accuracy

Prepare two separate fictional case sheets. Present them with appropriate technical language and respond to follow-up questions. Check that information has not moved from one case to the other.

SBAR can help organise a practice presentation, but is not a mandatory official rubric. Practise saying that information is missing rather than guessing it.

For writing, use the anamnesis-task guide. Compare each draft against its own video source.

Week 7: Timed task practice

Use the provider's current practice test and timing instructions. Practise the oral situations and the full written task, including video time. Gradually combine tasks while retaining separate notes and sources.

If errors increase sharply under time pressure, return to a shorter section and improve it before repeating the whole task. A shorter exercise is useful training but should not be labelled a full exam simulation.

Week 8: Review and exam-day preparation

Complete a practice run with the published task structure. Review information transfer, intelligibility, interaction and writing accuracy. Choose targeted corrections rather than adding many new topics.

Check your actual booking confirmation for location, start time, identification and arrival instructions. Keep the day before manageable. Eight weeks of practice does not establish that every learner is ready.
